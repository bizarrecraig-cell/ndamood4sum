Late Night Train Ride

Straight · Public · ~1,200 words

The last train of the night was nearly empty. He sat across from her, and the way he looked at her made the whole car feel smaller. By the next stop, neither of them was pretending anymore…

The carriage lights flickered as the train pulled out of the station. Most of the seats were empty. She had her headphones in but wasn’t really listening. He was watching her reflection in the dark window.

When their eyes finally met, the silence between them felt charged. He moved to the seat beside her without a word. Her hand found his thigh. His fingers slid under the hem of her skirt. The next station was still eight minutes away.

They didn’t speak. They didn’t need to. By the time the doors opened, both of them were breathing harder and smiling like they shared a secret the rest of the world would never know.

— End —

The Neighbor’s Secret

Straight · Neighbors · ~1,400 words

She only meant to borrow sugar. He only meant to be polite. Neither of them expected the door to stay open long after the sugar was gone…

The knock was light. He opened the door in a t-shirt and sweats. She held an empty measuring cup and an apology for the late hour.

“Sugar?” he asked, already smiling. She nodded. He stepped aside to let her in. The kitchen was small. The air between them wasn’t.

They talked about the building, the weather, nothing that mattered. Then she set the cup down and didn’t pick it up again. His hand found the small of her back. She rose onto her toes. The rest of the night was measured in quiet sounds and the glow of the stove light left on.

— End —

Locker Room Heat

Gay · Sports · ~1,100 words

Practice had been long. The showers were almost empty. Two teammates stayed behind for reasons that had nothing to do with conditioning…

Steam filled the tiled room. Most of the team had already left. He stayed under the water longer than usual, waiting. The other man walked in, towel low on his hips, and locked eyes with him.

No words at first. Just the sound of water and the knowledge that the door to the hallway was still unlocked. Hands found wet skin. Mouths followed. The risk made everything sharper.

When it was over they rinsed off in silence, dressed, and walked out like nothing had happened. Both of them knew it would happen again.

— End —

Hotel Key Exchange

Bi · Travel · ~1,300 words

A conference hotel, a wrong key card, and two people who decided not to correct the mistake…

The card didn’t open her door. She tried the next room down the hall out of frustration and it worked. He was already inside, half-dressed, looking as surprised as she was.

They could have laughed it off and called the front desk. Instead she stepped in and let the door close behind her. The city lights through the window were the only illumination they needed.

By morning the key cards had been sorted out. Neither of them mentioned the night when they passed each other in the lobby later that day. The smile they shared said enough.

— End —
